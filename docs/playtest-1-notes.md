# Playtest 1: notes for the design doc (2026-11-26)

JD played the first throwaway spike on his iPhone without reading the instructions.

## What confused him
- He didn't know what to do. The game has to teach itself.
- Serve and spike swipes: does the swipe go from the player to the spot, or on the spot, or anywhere? It was ambiguous.
- Too much yellow. The timing ring and the "you" ring under the player looked alike.
- Outside and opposite were on the wrong sides. From your team's view facing the net, the outside is on the left and the opposite is on the right.

## Ideas for the full game (not for the spike)
- **Tutorial in the full game** that uses bullet-time slowdowns and pauses to teach each touch.
- **Huddle and play calls.** Before each play the team huddles and the setter picks a play for each attacker, with defaults. The AI runs toward that play. You can change it mid-match, reading where their blockers are or how good the pass was. Make it run the way the high-level men's game does.
- **Defensive read.** When the ball is coming back over, swipe or tap toward the hitter you think will get it. That shifts your block and defense.

## Changes made to the spike for playtest 2
- Outside on the left (far side of the screen), opposite on the right (near side). The set compass now matches where they stand.
- Serve and spike aim by location: put your finger on their court where you want the ball, drag to adjust, let go to hit. Letting go is the timing. Aim near the net for a tip.
- Serve is one motion: hold to toss, drag to aim, let go at the top.
- No more yellow: a white arrow over your player, a white timing ring that turns green, white set labels.
